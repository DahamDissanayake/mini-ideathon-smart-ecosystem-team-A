LetGo Band Deliverable 02 SDG 3 · Good Health and Well-being Tier 1 · runs on the edge gateway

Five agents, one gateway, and no way around the Safeguard.

This is the agentic AI OS behind LetGo Band. Five autonomous agents share one daycare facility, run side by side on a single edge gateway, and are arbitrated by a deterministic policy engine that none of them can reach around.

Nothing here calls a model in a data centre. The whole loop closes inside the building, in under a second, and it closes on a person.

01 · The workflow

The decision loop

The system runs the same six-step loop continuously, once per feature window per band, all day. The numbering is the sequence, not decoration: each step can only act on what the step before it produced.

01
Sense
Bands broadcast into the room. Anchors hear whatever is in range and forward it with their own received signal strength. Neither tier decides anything, and neither holds a key.
no autonomy, by design
02
Ground
The gateway authenticates every advertisement, resolves which band it is, collapses the copies several anchors heard into one observation, and turns the signal strengths into a zone — or into unknown.
deterministic
03
Reason
Five agents read only the fields their contracts name and each answers one question. They run concurrently and never call one another.
autonomous, bounded
04
Arbitrate
Every proposal from every agent reaches the Safeguard. It approves as urgent, approves as routine, or rejects and logs. Conflicts resolve on a fixed precedence order, never ad hoc.
deterministic
05
Act
Approved output reaches a ranked worklist on the local console, or the once-a-day cloud summary. Rejected output is logged where an auditor can find it, not discarded.
routing only
06
A human decides
A carer acts. The system has taken no physical action, and cannot: there is no lock it can close, no door it can open, no alarm it can sound on its own authority.
the human is the actuator
02 · Multi-agent roles

What each agent reads, and what it may never do

Each agent owns exactly one question, subscribes to a named set of fields, and publishes one kind of output. The field-level isolation is enforced at the message bus rather than by convention, so an agent reading outside its contract is refused rather than reviewed.

Anomaly Monitor

Deterministic

“Does someone need to go and look, right now?”

Reads
Strap events, link state, motion, anchor coverage
Emits
Graded anomaly events: strap breach, signal loss after check-in, early power-off, prolonged stillness inconsistent with wear
May notSuppress a strap breach event under any circumstance. Breaches always escalate. It may not raise or withhold a safety escalation on zone confidence alone.

Attendance Manager

Deterministic

“Who is here, and do the four sources agree?”

Reads
Clip events, motion confirmation, presence via anchor coverage, the daily roster
Emits
Attendance state transitions including perimeter crossing, and the mismatches between the four sources
May notMark a child present on clip state alone. A band clipped around a chair leg closes the same circuit.

Movement Classifier

Model-backed

“What is this child doing?”

Reads
Feature windows: mean acceleration magnitude, variance, step count, cadence, posture estimate
Emits
Activity classifications with confidence scores
May notInterpret clinically, make recommendations, or output anything about a child’s development.

Day Summariser

Deterministic

“What did the day amount to?”

Reads
Classifications across a session, and the zone assignments they happened in
Emits
Active minutes, movement variety across activity classes and zones, fitness session participation
May notProduce calorie, weight, body composition, or fitness-scoring output. Never compares one child to another.

Trend Analyst

Model-backed

“Has this child drifted from their own baseline?”

Reads
Multi-day summaries, and per-day zone occupancy for that child
Emits
Activity pattern deviation flags, including zone preference deviation over weeks
May notProduce a conclusion, diagnosis, or characterisation of a child. Output is a flag for staff review only, never surfaced to parents unreviewed.

Why an agent, not a rule?

Three of the five are deterministic. So why call them agents at all?

Because
The agent boundary is a contract boundary, not a claim about how clever the thing inside is.
It buys
Field-level isolation — the Day Summariser cannot read strap state, so no summary bug can suppress a breach.
And
Independent failure, and a may not clause with a named owner, which is a test somebody can write.
03 · How the agents manage the facility

The autonomy is deliberately narrow, and the boundary is the point

Three layers of authority, and each one can only do the thing named on it. Read downward: the further you go, the more consequential the decision and the less of it belongs to software.

Layer 1 · The agents

Decide what deserves attention

They classify, reconcile, detect and flag, continuously, whether or not anyone is looking. No carer asked for any of it. That is the real autonomy in this system — and it is the whole of it.

Layer 2 · The Safeguard

Decides what reaches a person

No agent talks to another agent, writes to the console, or reaches the cloud. Every output goes to the Safeguard and stops there until it rules. There is no message path that skips it, no configuration flag that creates one, and no staff role that can bypass it. If a future feature needs an exception, the rule changes and the change is reviewed.

Layer 3 · A person

Decides what happens

The final actuator is always a carer walking to a room. The system has no lock it can close, no door it can open, and no alarm it can sound on its own authority. This is why zone data is allowed to rank an alert but never to trigger one by itself.

Carers can silence, dismiss, or escalate an alert the Safeguard has already approved. That is a human action on an approved alert, it is logged, and the alert is not deleted. It does not override the Safeguard, and it does not retrain anything.
04 · Decision-making under conflict

When two agents want the same carer at once

Safety outranks summary, always. When agent outputs disagree, or several want attention simultaneously, the Safeguard applies this order — highest first, and fixed in advance rather than negotiated at run time.

1
Strap breach, and unaccompanied perimeter crossing When both fire for the same child, the breach shows first — it is deterministic, and the crossing is probabilistic.
bypasses batching
2
Other safety anomalies Signal loss after check-in, early device power-off, prolonged stillness inconsistent with wear, battery critical.
urgent
3
Attendance state and reconciliation mismatches Including a clip that never confirmed with wear-consistent motion.
urgent
4
Day summary output The only class that routinely leaves the building.
routine
5
Trend flags Queued for staff review. Never escalated as an alert, never surfaced to a parent unreviewed.
queued
▸
Attendance never wins over safety. A child recorded as CHECKED_OUT whose band reports a strap breach still generates the breach.
▸
The classifier never gates a safety event. Anomaly Monitor conclusions that rest on strap state or link state stand on their own, whatever the Movement Classifier says or fails to say.
▸
Zone never stands alone. A perimeter crossing alert carries the evidence it was raised on — last strap state, last motion, which anchors last heard the band — and its confidence value. An alert that cannot carry that evidence is still raised, and marked uncorroborated.
▸
Below the confidence floor, the Safeguard suppresses and logs. It does not guess, and it does not surface a hedged result. The system fails to unknown rather than to a plausible answer.
The signal trust hierarchy

Not every signal is worth the same

The three signals this system runs on are not equally trustworthy, and the Safeguard ranks them explicitly rather than treating any reading as a fact.

Signal
Character

1
Strap circuit

A closed circuit is measured, not inferred. It is the authority on wear, and no classifier output can contradict it.
